Forgotten Composers British Orchestral Music 1850-1950

Welcome

Welcome to this blog, which explores British orchestral music written between 1850 and 1950. My focus is on composers whose work has sadly fallen into oblivion. By searching for orchestral scores across a multitude of sources and inputting many notes using Sibelius and Noteperformer software I have created high quality renditions of their work so we can at last hear the actual music as it was intended; music which has for too long been forgotten, and in some cases never performed.

Arthur Hervey(1855-1922)

- October 21, 2026
Arthur Hervey “Writer, critic, promoter and composer” Hervey was born in Paris to Irish parents who were rich landowners in Ireland. Their wealth  facilitated their travels across Europe and they enjoyed cultural pursuits such as music and literature. This privileged background shaped Hervey's formative years before the family relocated to England. He was educated at the Oratory, Birmingham, and studied privately in London with Berthold Tours and Edouard Marlois, two organist-composers with a French background. He spent fairly equal time as both a composer and writer, prinipally biographical studies of composers, most of which were devoted to French contemporaries like Saint-Saëns and Bruneau. He was widely regarded as an expert on French music. As music critic for Vanity Fair from 1889 to 1892 and subsequently for The Morning Post from 1892 to 1908, Hervey penned weekly columns and concert reviews that promoted emerging European talent to British audiences. Percy Godfrey (1859-1945)

- October 20, 2026
Percy Godfrey “Prizewinner and a slide from the limelight.” Godfrey began his studies at the University of Durham and then at the Royal Academy of Music, where his teachers included Sir George Macfarren and Ebenezer Prout. The Dutch violinist and composer Berthold Tours, who became a close friend, was also an important early influence. After graduating he became a schoolmaster in Bengeo, Hertford before being appointed music-master and organist at The King's School, Canterbury. He conducted the East Kent Orchestra. Walter Macfarren(1826-1905)

- October 14, 2026
As a composer, Walter Macfarren (1826-1905) was arguably very much in his brother's shadow but we must be aware that he had a substantial reputation of his own. George Alexander Macfarren His elder brother, Sir George Alexander Macfarren (20 years his senior), held the more prominent public positions: Principal of the Royal Academy of Music and Professor of Music at Cambridge. That along with his large output of orchestral music including 9 symphonies, helps explain why George’s name dominated contemporary musical life.  Walter’s distinction lay particularly in piano teaching, performing and editing. He taught at the Academy from 1846 to 1903, and his pupils included Tobias Matthay and Henry Wood. He was a respected musician, rather than simply an overlooked younger brother.  Orchestral work list: Only those in bold have surviving scores.
