Forgotten Composers British Orchestral Music 1850-1950

Welcome

Welcome to this blog, which explores British orchestral music written between 1850 and 1950. My focus is on composers whose work has sadly fallen into oblivion. By searching for orchestral scores across a multitude of sources and inputting many notes using Sibelius and Noteperformer software I have created high quality renditions of their work so we can at last hear the actual music as it was intended; music which has for too long been forgotten, and in some cases never performed.

R. O. Morris (1886-1948)

- October 22, 2026

“Master of counterpoint, forgotten composer”

Reginald Owen Morris was born in York in 1886 and educated at Harrow, New College, Oxford, and the Royal College of Music. Known to colleagues and friends simply as R. O., he became an influential teacher of composition and counterpoint. His pupils included Gerald Finzi, Michael Tippett, Constant Lambert and Edmund Rubbra.

During the First World War he served in the Duke of Cornwall’s Light Infantry. He returned to the Royal College of Music as a professor in 1920, spending two years at the Curtis Institute in Philadelphia from 1926 before resuming his London teaching. His books, including Contrapuntal Technique in the Sixteenth Century, helped establish his lasting reputation.

Morris’s own music has received far less attention. His output includes symphonies, concertante works and music for strings, much of it written during a concentrated period in the 1920s and early 1930s. The surviving scores reveal the composer behind the teacher, with independent musical lines playing an important part in his orchestral thinking.

Sinfonia in C major (1928–1929)

Morris’s Sinfonia in C major, also known as A Little Symphony, is a four-movement orchestral work composed in 1928–1929 and dedicated to Arthur Bliss.

The autograph full score survives in the Royal College of Music Library as MS 4443 and has been digitised. It provides a direct route into Morris’s orchestral writing during one of his most productive periods as a composer.
