Forgotten Composers British Orchestral Music 1850-1950

Welcome

Welcome to this blog, which explores British orchestral music written between 1850 and 1950. My focus is on composers whose work has sadly fallen into oblivion. By searching for orchestral scores from various sources I have hopefully created decent renditions of their music using Sibelius and Noteperformer software.

We can at last hear their music brought back to life; music which has for too long been forgotten, and in some cases never performed.

Charles Oberthür (1819-1895)

“Orchestral scores amidst a sea of music for the harp”

Charles Oberthür was born in Munich in 1819 and made London his home in 1844. His career began in continental theatre orchestras, with engagements in Zürich, Wiesbaden and Mannheim, before he moved to England. In London he initially played at the Italian Opera, then concentrated on teaching, composition and appearances as a soloist.

He became a professor of harp at the Royal Academy of Music in 1861. His enormous catalogue is dominated by music for or involving his own instrument, ranging from teaching pieces and arrangements to chamber works and concert music. That reputation can obscure the wider ambitions of a composer who also wrote operatic, choral and orchestral works.

Oberthür’s place in this blog rests on his long contribution to musical life in Britain and on the orchestral music that lies beyond his familiar harp repertoire. His concert works draw on literary and legendary subjects, including Rübezahl, Loreley and Shakespeare.

Vorspiel zu ‘Shakespeare, ein Winternachtstraum’, Op. 210

This prelude for large orchestra was written for Carl Kösting’s dramatic poem Shakespeare, ein Winternachtstraum. Its title refers to Kösting’s work about Shakespeare, rather than to a play of that name by Shakespeare himself.

August Cranz of Hamburg published the orchestral score and parts in 1885; both were advertised in Hofmeister’s music catalogue that March. The surviving score offers a practical starting point for exploring Oberthür as an orchestral composer. It is dedicated to Victoria, Crown Princess of Germany and Prussia.
