Forgotten Composers British Orchestral Music 1850-1950

Welcome

Welcome to this blog, which explores British orchestral music written between 1850 and 1950. My focus is on composers whose work has sadly fallen into oblivion. By searching for orchestral scores from various sources I have hopefully created decent renditions of their music using Sibelius and Noteperformer software.

We can at last hear their music brought back to life; music which has for too long been forgotten, and in some cases never performed.

Tobias Matthay (1858-1945)

“Orchestral secrets from a master of the piano”

Tobias Matthay is remembered above all as a piano teacher and writer, but his compositions deserve a place alongside that reputation. Born in London in 1858, he studied piano with Walter Macfarren at the Royal Academy of Music, and composition with William Sterndale Bennett and Arthur Sullivan. The connection with Macfarren provides a direct link with another composer explored in this blog.

Matthay became a sub-professor at the Academy in 1876 and a full professor in 1880, remaining on its staff until 1925. His pupils included Myra Hess, Harriet Cohen, Clifford Curzon and Moura Lympany. He opened the Tobias Matthay Piano School in 1905, while continuing his Academy teaching. His influential book The Act of Touch in All Its Diversity had appeared in 1903.

The fame of the teacher has tended to overshadow the composer. His orchestral output includes two symphonies, concert overtures and works for piano and orchestra, written largely during his early career. These pieces offer another way of approaching a musician whose name is now so closely associated with piano technique.
